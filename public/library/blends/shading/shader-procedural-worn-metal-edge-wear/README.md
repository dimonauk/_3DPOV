# Procedural Worn Metal — Bevel-Shader Edge Wear, Noise Bump & Roughness Gradient

**Blender 5.1 · Shading · EEVEE Next**

A fully procedural worn-metal material that concentrates surface damage
exactly where physics places it: at sharp edges and high-traffic corners.
No UV unwrap required.  No texture files.  One material node tree.

## Technique

The **Bevel node** is an EEVEE-exclusive screen-space operator.  It reads
the G-buffer depth to estimate curvature at each pixel and returns a Normal
that tilts toward the adjacent face at tight-angle edges.  Comparing this
perturbed Normal against the unmodified Geometry Normal via a DOT PRODUCT
yields a curvature scalar — 1 on flat faces, falling toward 0 at edges.

Inverting that scalar gives an **edge-wear mask** that drives a Mix Shader
between clean polished steel and oxidised scratched metal.  A second signal
from a Noise texture adds random wear patches on flat surfaces.  A MAX node
combines both: either factor alone is enough to trigger wear, but they cannot
compound and over-dirty mid-face zones.

Micro-scratch detail comes from a Bump node fed by the same Noise texture,
wired into the Worn PBSDF's Normal socket.  Bump perturbs the shading normal
without modifying geometry — the silhouette stays crisp.

## EEVEE limitation for WebXR

The Bevel node's curvature detection does not export to GLB.  The GLB carries
only the flat Metallic / Roughness value from the blend of clean and worn
PBSDF at the Blender export moment.  For full wear detail in Three.js, bake
the Roughness pass in Cycles before export.
