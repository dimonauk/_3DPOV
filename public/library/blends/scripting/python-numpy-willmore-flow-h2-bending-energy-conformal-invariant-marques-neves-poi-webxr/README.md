# Willmore Flow — H² Bending Energy, Conformal Invariance & Marques-Neves Theorem

**Topic**: Differential geometry · Energy functionals · Discrete PDEs  
**Blender version**: 5.1  
**Licence**: CC0-1.0  
**Mesh**: 1 800 vertices, 7 200 triangles (60 × 30 torus grid, triangulated)

---

## What it is

The **Willmore energy** of a smooth closed surface Σ is:

```
W[Σ] = ∫_Σ H² dA
```

where H = (κ₁ + κ₂)/2 is the mean curvature and dA is the area element.
This is an intrinsic measure of how much a surface bends beyond its topology
demands — a round sphere has W = 4π (the lowest possible value for *any*
immersed surface, proved by Li & Yau 1982), and the famous Willmore conjecture
(now a theorem, Marques & Neves 2012) states that the minimum over all
**embedded tori** is W = 2π² ≈ 19.739, achieved by the stereographic
projection of the Clifford torus from S³.

---

## Why it matters

**Conformal invariance** is the key property: W is invariant under Möbius
transformations of R³ ∪ {∞}.  Möbius transformations are generated by
Euclidean isometries plus inversion x ↦ x/|x|².  Because H dA is conformally
invariant (H rescales as 1/λ under scaling, dA rescales as λ², so H² dA is
scale-invariant; inversion adds a correction exactly cancelled by the change in
normal direction), one can move between geometrically equivalent representatives
freely without changing W.  This is why the Clifford torus in S³ and its
stereographic image in R³ have the same W.

**Marques & Neves (2012)** proved the conjecture using Almgren-Pitts min-max
theory applied to the space of embedded surfaces in S³.  The proof was a
landmark because it required treating the space of 4-cycles, not just
2-cycles, and showed that the Clifford torus is the unique minimax surface
(up to Möbius equivalence) in that space.

## Discrete implementation

The discrete Willmore flow is a fourth-order PDE in disguise.  The linearised
gradient of W at vertex v is:

```
∇_v W ≈ -ΔH · n̂_v   (leading term; full gradient adds 2H(H²−K)n̂)
```

Two applications of the cotangent Laplacian are needed:

1. **Pass 1**: `L_cot(X) / (2A)` → mean-curvature vector H⃗, then `|H⃗|` → H scalar
2. **Pass 2**: `L_cot(H) / (2A)` → ΔH scalar

Then: `X ← X − dt · ΔH · n̂`

The stability criterion is dt ∼ h⁴ (biharmonic), far more restrictive than
MCF's dt ∼ h².  With mesh spacing h ≈ 0.13 m, the script uses dt = 8 × 10⁻⁶.
