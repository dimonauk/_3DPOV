## What this is

The **Cayley Nodal Cubic** is the unique (up to projective equivalence) degree-3 algebraic
surface in ℙ³ that achieves the maximum number of ordinary double points for a cubic: **4 nodes**.
Proved by Arthur Cayley and George Salmon in 1849, it anchors the bottom of the node-count
record table for algebraic surfaces:

| Degree | Name | Max nodes | Studio entry |
|--------|------|-----------|--------------|
| 3 | **Cayley nodal cubic** | **4** | **this entry** |
| 4 | Kummer quartic | 16 | `python-numpy-kummer-quartic-16-nodes-…` |
| 5 | Togliatti quintic | 31 | — |
| 6 | Barth sextic | 65 | `python-numpy-barth-sextic-65-nodes-…` |

The implicit equation (projective):

```
e₃(x₀,x₁,x₂,x₃) = x₀x₁x₂ + x₀x₁x₃ + x₀x₂x₃ + x₁x₂x₃ = 0
```

Affine (x₃ = 1):

```
f(x,y,z) = xyz + xy + xz + yz = 0
```

equivalently:  `(1+x)(1+y)(1+z) = 1 + x + y + z`

---

## The 4 Nodes

Nodes are at the 4 projective coordinate vertices N₀…N₃, permuted by the symmetry group **S₄**
(order 24 — all permutations of 4 elements). In the affine patch, only N₃ = (0,0,0) is
visible; the other three lie at projective infinity along the coordinate axes.

At the origin node, the quadratic part of f is:

```
Q(x,y,z) = xy + xz + yz
```

with Hessian eigenvalues +½ (once), −¼ (twice) → signature **(1,2)** → real quadric cone
with two nappes. This is the minimal singularity type, **A₁** (ordinary double point).

---

## The 9 Lines

A smooth cubic surface has exactly 27 lines (Cayley–Salmon 1849).
The Cayley nodal cubic, with 4 nodes, has exactly **9 lines** (Segre 1887):

**6 edge lines** — connecting pairs of nodes (tetrahedron edges):
- In affine x₃=1: the x-axis, y-axis, z-axis are visible (3 edges through N₃)
- 3 further edges between N₀,N₁,N₂ lie at projective infinity

**3 non-edge lines** — found via the factorisation `f = x₀x₁(x₂+x₃) + x₂x₃(x₀+x₁)`:
- **L_A**: `x₀+x₁=0, x₂+x₃=0` → affine: y = −x, z = −1
- **L_B**: `x₀+x₂=0, x₁+x₃=0` → affine: z = −x, y = −1
- **L_C**: `x₀+x₃=0, x₁+x₂=0` → affine: x = −1, y = −z

These 3 non-edge lines are NOT through any node — they connect the 3 Sylow 2-subgroups
of S₄ (each D₄ of order 8 stabilises one line).
