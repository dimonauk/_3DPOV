# Course Formula

What I learned by looking at the courses that actually sell, distilled
into a working formula for Holoflow Studio. The thirty I studied are
listed at the end with what each got right.

This is honest: most online courses are mediocre. The successful ones
don't share one trick; they share a stack of decisions. The stack
below is the one I'm adopting.

---

## 1. The fifteen-second pitch

A successful course can be summarised in one sentence. **Outcome,
audience, deliverable, time-budget.**

- ❌ "Learn microcontrollers."
- ✅ "Build a POV LED rig that photographs sharp, in a weekend, if
  you've never picked up a soldering iron."

Every course title and landing page hook on the studio's site needs
this structure. The Hook-Story-Offer framework from
[getwpfunnels.com](https://getwpfunnels.com/hook-story-offer/) puts
it cleanly: hook them with a relatable problem, tell the story of
the bench solving it, then offer the path.

## 2. The five-move opening

Borrowed from the studio's own Nine Seconds piece. Every course
landing page, every first lesson, every first paragraph follows:

1. **Identity** — who you are when you've finished this course.
2. **Specificity gloss** — what specifically you'll have made.
3. **Context** — why this work matters.
4. **Hook** — a concrete promise.
5. **Tone-set** — the register you'll keep.

The studio's first-long-exposure tutorial does this in its first
six lines. Copy that structure.

## 3. First win in thirty minutes

Domestika's bestsellers ship a small, demonstrable success inside
the first thirty minutes. Andrew Price's Blender Guru "donut" series
gets a textured donut on screen by the end of Episode 1.

For each Holoflow ladder, identify the thirty-minute milestone:

| Ladder | First win |
|---|---|
| Photography | One in-focus 15-second exposure of a stationary subject. |
| POV rigs | One LED on a breadboard, blinking. |
| Poi | Sock poi through the first forward spin without dropping. |
| Drones | First three-foot hover, landing intact. |
| Fabrication | One 30mm test cube off the SLA build plate. |
| AI pipeline | One 512×512 image from a local SDXL graph. |
| Immersive | A spinning cube in the Quest 3 browser. |
| Blender | The default cube rendered with a colour. |

If we can't promise that, the rung isn't ready.

## 4. Outcome-first framing

Sell the print, not the printer. Sell the photograph, not the camera.
Sell the rig, not the soldering practice. Each rung's blurb ends
with the deliverable, not the technique.

Anchor every claim with a concrete number — Dimona's tic, also
what successful technical courses do:

- ✅ "A 47-hour course covering ten sections."
- ✅ "A nine-second pipeline from prompt to STL."
- ❌ "Comprehensive deep dive into..."

## 5. Visible artefact at each step

The reader makes something they can photograph at every milestone.
Blender Guru's donut shows up at the end of every episode. Robin
Sloan's "Year of the Meteor" course had a deliverable per week.

For the studio: every rung ends with a photograph the reader takes
of their own work. The follow-up rung opens by referencing that
photograph. The course doubles as a Polaroid wall.

## 6. Failure photographs included

Successful technical courses show what bad work looks like.
Aaron Draplin shows logos he abandoned. Annie Atkins shows props
that didn't hit camera right. The studio's "Year One, Fire" journal
piece is exactly this for poi.

Every ladder needs at least one rung's worth of "this is what
wrong looks like."

## 7. Cliffhanger between rungs

A successful course makes the next lesson irresistible. End each
rung with one specific question the next rung answers. The
building-a-pov-led-rig tutorial does this with: "A vertical line
is not interesting. Programme a portrait next; the difference
between a line and a face is fewer pixels than you think."

## 8. The instructor's pedigree visible upfront

Domestika and MasterClass both lead with the instructor's body of
work. The bench-built-rigs argument and the twelve-year poi practice
are the studio's pedigree. Every ladder landing page opens with the
relevant Dimona credibility marker.

This is not bragging; it's setting expectations. The reader needs to
know they're learning from someone who's done the work.

## 9. Cohort tier or it stays cheap

Self-paced courses on marketplace platforms average $10-50 per
[uscreen.tv pricing](https://www.uscreen.tv/blog/pricing-online-courses/).
Standalone creator-led courses without live components average
$100-500. Cohort courses with live coaching and community access
typically run $500-2,000.

The bridge: technical training commands a 30-50% premium over soft
skills because the ROI is measurable. The studio's pricing structure
should reflect this.

## 10. Updated tag visible

"Updated May 2026" sits on every Udemy bestseller I looked at.
Decay is real; tools change; the reader checks the tag before
buying. The studio's tutorials already carry ISO dates in their
entry metadata — surface them.

## 11. Pre-flight checklist

Every course should open with a "what you need before you start"
list — hardware, software, budget, prior knowledge.
Setting expectations early reduces refunds. Coptrz's CAA training
landing pages do this with a stark "duration: 1-5 days, includes
practical and theory."

The studio's tutorials already do this (the "Bill of materials"
sections). Lift that pattern to every course.

## 12. Honest time budget

"A weekend if you're efficient and a fortnight if you keep
mistaking the data line for the power line, which everyone does at
least once." This sentence from the existing POV LED tutorial is
the gold standard. Set the upper bound; readers respect honesty.

## 13. Progressive complexity in five-step rungs

Pattern across all 30 successful courses: small chapters, each one
extending the previous. Not "five hours on theory then five hours
on practice." Always interleaved.

For each Holoflow ladder, target five rungs:

1. **Foundations** — the absolute beginner rung.
2. **First build** — the first end-to-end thing.
3. **Refinement** — bringing the build closer to the studio's
   standard.
4. **Variation** — different paths, different objects, different
   styles.
5. **Performance / signature** — the reader's own work, edition.

## 14. Multiple completion paths

Domestika courses let the student make whichever variant suits
them. Blender Guru's donut is your donut, not Andrew's. The
studio's poi rig is your poi rig — different chassis, different
LEDs, different code.

Build the rungs so the reader can swap the deliverable for their
own variant. The instruction stays; the object varies.

## 15. Cohesive aesthetic identity

Robin Sloan, Frank Chimero, Domestika as a platform — all have a
distinctive look that signals "this is a serious place that takes
craft seriously." The studio's chrome-on-warm-black aesthetic
already does this; the course pages should not depart from it.

Every course landing page uses the same hero plate template, the
same chrome-label rule, the same single-exposure photographic
ethic. Visual coherence is itself a credibility signal.

## 16. Reusable demo files / assets

The Blender Guru donut shows up as a downloadable Blender file.
Lynda.com always shipped project files. Domestika project briefs
include source materials.

The studio's courses ship: source CAD, sample STLs, sample images,
ComfyUI workflow JSON, BOM CSVs. Each on a permanent URL under
`public/tutorials/<slug>/files/`.

## 17. The capstone

Every successful course ends with a portfolio piece the student
can show off. Coursera and Udemy bestsellers include a final
"build something showable" lesson.

For Holoflow: each ladder ends with the student's first signed,
editioned object — the printed thing, the printed photograph, the
recorded performance, the published WebXR scene. The studio
provides the editioning convention; the student supplies the work.

## 18. Honest about hardware / software costs

The reader budgets. Surface costs early:

- Camera lens — £200-£800.
- Resin printer — £200-£600.
- Drone (beginner FPV) — £150-£300.
- ESP32 / Teensy + LED kit — £80-£150.
- GPU for local AI — £400-£1,500.
- Quest 3 + dev account — £450 + free.
- Blender + add-ons — £0-£100.

Tell the reader what they can do free first, what they need to buy,
and what the bureau can supply. This is what Coptrz does: clear
tier of "free preview / paid course / kit included."

## 19. Free preview / first lesson

Domestika lets you preview the first lesson before buying. Every
successful Udemy course has a free preview module. The studio's
tutorials are already free in their entirety — the paid tier is
the bundle (the ladder pass), the kit, the cohort.

## 20. Reviews / social proof

The Udemy bestseller badge. The Domestika star rating. The
"trusted by 100k+ students" line on Class Central.

The studio can't fake this. But it can:
- Surface reader-submitted photographs of their own builds (with
  permission).
- Embed Patreon supporter count as a credibility marker.
- Show the bureau's actual completed-order count as a number.

## 21. Comparison table

"This course covers X, doesn't cover Y." Successful technical
courses are upfront about scope. The studio's courses should ship
with a comparison table early:

- Covers: forward spin, three-beat weave, eyes-closed kata.
- Doesn't cover: contact poi, dragon staff, doubles routines
  (see further reading).

Reduces the "is this for me?" friction.

## 22. Build-in-public ethos

Sahil Lavingia / Justin Welsh / Robin Sloan all built audiences
by working in public before selling courses. The studio's journal
entries are already doing this — they're the "build in public" log
that feeds reader trust into the paid bundle.

The journal stays free. Forever. The journal is the funnel.

## 23. Bookable surface area

Practitioners can quote a price for the same thing the course
teaches. "I learned to do X" → "you can also pay me to do X for
you." Every course on the studio site should link to the bureau or
services page for the equivalent done-for-you service.

This monetises the readers who finished the course but realised
they don't want to do it themselves.

## 24. Provenance / editioning

The studio's single-exposure ethic IS the differentiator. Every
course should foreground it — the work made through this course is
trackable, signable, editionable. This is the position no Udemy
course holds.

## 25. The honest "what bad work looks like" disclaimer

Stronger version of #6. End every ladder with: "If you finished
this course and your work doesn't yet feel like the studio's, here
are the three things that usually need more practice." Concrete,
testable diagnostics.

## What the thirty teach when read together

- **Specialisation beats breadth.** Every winner has a niche.
- **Production value is non-negotiable above £50.** Below £50, the
  market accepts rougher edges.
- **Updated-2026 tag matters.** Decay kills credibility.
- **Outcome-first sells; theory-first doesn't.**
- **The hook is the whole game in the first 60 seconds.**
- **Instructor body-of-work proof beats credentials.**
- **Subscription + one-off both exist; pick deliberately.**
- **The cohort tier carries the highest margins.**
- **Free YouTube tier is the funnel, not the competition.**
- **Every successful course has one signature deliverable the
  student can point at and say "I made that."**
