# Learning Psychology — The Light Pattern

The studio's position: the psychology of learning is a tool. It can
be used to keep people clicking past the point of value (the dark
pattern — Candy Crush, the slot machine, the infinite-scroll feed)
or to keep people learning past the point they'd otherwise give up
(the light pattern — Duolingo done right, every good piano teacher,
every dojo).

Holoflow's courses use the light pattern. The reader keeps going
because each step delivers a real "aha", and because the studio has
engineered the path so the aha lands at the moment the reader
needs it. No fake progress bars, no streak guilt, no email
manipulation. Just the small wins, in the right order, at the right
density.

This document is the framework. Every tutorial in every ladder is
built against the rules below.

---

## The first principle

A learner continues for one of two reasons:

1. **They are mid-flow** — the next step is just challenging enough
   to engage but not so hard they stall. (Csikszentmihalyi.
   Standard flow theory.)
2. **They just had an aha** — the dopamine hit from a successful
   small win, fresh in working memory, makes the next step feel
   like more of the same. (Variable reward schedules, applied
   ethically.)

A learner stops for one of three reasons:

1. **Stalled** — the gap between what they can do and what the
   next step demands is too wide.
2. **Bored** — the gap is too narrow; they're not learning anything.
3. **Lost the thread** — they don't remember why they're doing this.

Every tutorial is engineered to maximise (1) and (2) above and
minimise the three below. Every rung in every ladder is checked
against these five conditions.

## The eighteen levers

Eighteen techniques the studio applies. Each one has a known name
in the literature; each one has a concrete instruction for how to
use it in a Holoflow course; each one has a dark-pattern variant
the studio refuses to use.

### 1. The Zeigarnik effect (open loops)

**Mechanism.** Unfinished tasks occupy working memory more than
finished ones. The brain wants closure.

**Light use.** End each rung with one specific, named, unanswered
question that the next rung answers. "A vertical line is not
interesting. Programme a portrait next." Reader closes the laptop
holding that thread.

**Dark variant we refuse.** Cliffhanger followed by a paywall.
"To find out what happens next, upgrade now."

### 2. Variable rewards

**Mechanism.** Unpredictable timing of rewards triggers stronger
dopamine response than predictable timing.

**Light use.** Some rungs end with a small surprise — a bonus
"if you got this far, here's an additional thing you didn't
realise you'd unlocked": an extra LED test pattern, an extra
Blender shader, an extra ComfyUI workflow. Not every rung; not
predictably; not gameably.

**Dark variant we refuse.** Loot box mechanics. Random reward
withholding to drive engagement. Anything that resembles a slot
machine.

### 3. Mastery loops

**Mechanism.** Repeating a small skill until it becomes
unconscious creates the substrate for the next skill. Karate
katas, scales on piano, Domestika's "do the brief, then do the
brief differently."

**Light use.** Every ladder has a deliberate repetition rung —
"now do the same thing three more times, varying one parameter
each time." Each variant has the same scaffold but a different
output. The reader internalises the pattern.

**Dark variant we refuse.** Grinding for the sake of grinding.
Repetition with no variation isn't mastery; it's busywork.

### 4. Identity-based motivation

**Mechanism.** People follow through on actions consistent with
their self-concept. "I am the kind of person who finishes things"
is a stronger lever than "I should finish this thing."

**Light use.** Frame each ladder's outcome as identity, not skill:
"You are now someone who builds their own POV LED rigs." The
reader's identity has updated; the studio has held a mirror.

**Dark variant we refuse.** Aspirational sales copy designed to
make the reader feel small. "Are you tired of being someone who
can't…?" Manipulative; we don't.

### 5. The worked example effect

**Mechanism.** Novices learn more from studying a complete worked
example than from solving a problem cold. The cognitive load of
generating IS the bottleneck, not the cognitive load of reading.

**Light use.** Every rung opens with a complete worked example
walked through end-to-end before the reader is asked to do
anything. The studio's existing tutorials already do this — the
BOM walkthrough, the firmware walkthrough, the test walkthrough.

**Dark variant we refuse.** Withholding the worked example to
force the student to struggle. The struggle should come from the
EXTENSION, not the foundation.

### 6. Spaced repetition

**Mechanism.** Information reviewed at increasing intervals
(Ebbinghaus, then SuperMemo, then Anki) sticks better than
massed practice.

**Light use.** Each rung's opening references one concept from
two rungs prior, by name, briefly. "Remember the level shifter from
Rung 1? Same one applies here for the same reason." The reader's
recall is exercised without them noticing.

**Dark variant we refuse.** Daily-streak guilt notifications.
Duolingo's exclamation marks. The point of spaced repetition is
the spacing, not the anxiety.

### 7. Scaffolding (Vygotsky's ZPD)

**Mechanism.** Learning happens in the Zone of Proximal
Development — the gap between what the learner can do alone and
what they can do with support. Support is removed gradually.

**Light use.** Rung 1 supplies everything: the wiring diagram,
the firmware, the test pattern. Rung 4 supplies the framework but
expects the reader to write the firmware. Rung 5 expects the
reader to design the test pattern themselves. Scaffold gradually
removed.

**Dark variant we refuse.** Bait-and-switch difficulty. Easy
free preview, sudden steep paywall content. Difficulty curve
should be honest from start.

### 8. Concrete-first, abstract-later

**Mechanism.** Humans learn concepts faster from concrete
examples than from definitions. The pattern emerges from the
examples; the definition comes after.

**Light use.** Every concept in every rung is introduced with the
concrete first. "Wire this. Now look at what you wired. Here's
why that pattern is called a 'voltage divider' and where else
it shows up." Definitions come after the hands-on.

**Dark variant we refuse.** Theory-first lectures. The Khan
Academy mistake of explaining derivatives before showing what
they're for.

### 9. Curiosity gaps

**Mechanism.** A specific question with an obvious-but-unstated
answer creates a measurable pull (George Loewenstein's
information-gap theory). Trivia shows live on this.

**Light use.** Section openings pose a specific question whose
answer the section delivers. "Why does the WS2812 strip sometimes
work fine and sometimes produce nonsense? The reason is in the
clock domain." The reader keeps reading because the gap is open
and specific.

**Dark variant we refuse.** Clickbait. "You won't BELIEVE which
sensor every poi spinner gets wrong." The studio holds register.

### 10. The endowed-progress effect

**Mechanism.** People are more motivated to complete a task if
they perceive themselves as having already made progress. (Nunes
and Drèze; the "head start" coffee-card study.)

**Light use.** Every ladder shows the reader having already
completed a foundational step before the first rung. "You have a
camera — you've already completed the prerequisite of Owning a
Camera." The progress bar starts at 10%, not 0%, and the 10% is
honest.

**Dark variant we refuse.** Fake progress bars. Phantom
achievements. The progress shown must reflect real progress.

### 11. Optimal challenge (Csikszentmihalyi's flow)

**Mechanism.** Maximum engagement happens when challenge slightly
exceeds current skill — too easy and the learner disengages, too
hard and they stall.

**Light use.** Every rung is benchmarked against the typical
reader's skill at THAT point in the ladder. The first poi rung
expects no poi experience and gives a 100% achievable target.
The third rung expects the first two; gives a slightly stretched
target.

**Dark variant we refuse.** Difficulty inflation to extend
engagement. Adding artificial barriers (mandatory waits, paywalled
hints) to slow completion.

### 12. The Goldilocks zone (information density)

**Mechanism.** Too much info per page overwhelms; too little
bores. The sweet spot for technical instruction is roughly one
new concept per scroll-screen.

**Light use.** Tutorials are paragraphed so each new screen
introduces one named concept, lands one micro-aha, and primes
the next. Scroll discipline.

**Dark variant we refuse.** Dripping content slowly to inflate
session time. "Lesson 1 of 47" when 47 is artificial.

### 13. Social proof at the right moment

**Mechanism.** People follow what other people are doing —
especially at decision points. Cialdini.

**Light use.** Just before each ladder's final rung, surface
"X readers have completed this ladder. Y of them sent in photos
of their builds — see the gallery." Real numbers, real photos,
consent-gated.

**Dark variant we refuse.** Fake counts. "1,283 people viewing
this page right now." The studio holds honesty.

### 14. Implementation intentions

**Mechanism.** "I will do X at Y time in Z place" beats "I want
to do X." (Gollwitzer; the planning prompt effect.)

**Light use.** At the end of each rung, a small one-line prompt:
"When will you do the next rung? Pick a day this week." Reader
sets the implementation intention; not enforced; gentle.

**Dark variant we refuse.** Calendar bookings forced into the
flow. Notifications. Pestering. The intention is the reader's,
not the studio's.

### 15. Effortful retrieval (the testing effect)

**Mechanism.** Recalling information from memory strengthens it
more than re-reading. (Roediger and Karpicke.)

**Light use.** End of each rung: a single one-line reflection
prompt that requires the reader to recall, not look up. "Without
scrolling back, what's the role of the 1000µF capacitor?" The
answer is in the next paragraph; the recall is the value.

**Dark variant we refuse.** High-stakes quizzes. Gated tests that
block progress. Anxiety-induced retrieval that becomes its own
barrier.

### 16. Aesthetic identity / cohesive look

**Mechanism.** Visual coherence signals expertise. The reader's
brain trusts a well-designed surface more readily than a poorly
designed one — fair or not, this is the heuristic.

**Light use.** Every tutorial uses the chrome-on-warm-black
identity, the single-exposure photographic ethic, the
chrome-label dividers. The reader recognises the place. Trust
builds across tutorials.

**Dark variant we refuse.** Manufactured premium aesthetic to
mask weak content. The look reflects the work; the work IS the
work.

### 17. Reciprocity

**Mechanism.** People who have received freely-given value are
more likely to give in return. (Cialdini again.)

**Light use.** All seven existing ladders are free; the funnel
is reciprocity. The reader who has learned the entire WebXR
ladder for free is in a different relationship with the bureau
than a cold visitor. The studio is upfront about this: "This is
free because the studio benefits when readers become makers.
That includes the occasional reader buying from the bureau."

**Dark variant we refuse.** Manipulative free gifts. Free →
endless upsell sequence → drip campaigns. The free is genuinely
free.

### 18. The endowment effect (your work, your sculpture)

**Mechanism.** People value things more when they have made or
chosen them. (Thaler.)

**Light use.** Every ladder's final deliverable is the reader's
own variant. Their photograph, their rig, their print, their
WebXR scene. The bureau then offers to print their variant
specifically. The reader's investment in their own work becomes
the natural commerce path.

**Dark variant we refuse.** Fake personalisation. "Your custom
quote: £X." Anything where the personalisation is for
manipulation rather than for the reader.

## How the levers stack into a single rung

A well-built rung touches between five and eight of the eighteen.
Not all of them; that would be exhausting and obvious. A
representative rung structure:

1. **Hook (lever 9, curiosity gap)** — one specific question.
2. **Recall pivot (lever 6, spaced repetition)** — one named
   concept from two rungs prior.
3. **Worked example (lever 5)** — the studio doing it, with photos.
4. **Concrete-first walkthrough (lever 8)** — wire it, build it,
   do it.
5. **The first aha (lever 1, Zeigarnik open + small win)** — the
   reader's own version of the thing works.
6. **Scaffolded extension (lever 7)** — now do it without all the
   help.
7. **Reflection prompt (lever 15, retrieval)** — one question, no
   scroll.
8. **Cliffhanger (lever 1)** — the next rung's question, named.
9. **Implementation intention (lever 14)** — when will you do this?

That's a Holoflow rung. The reader closes the laptop having had
the aha, having strengthened the recall, holding the next thread.

## The dopamine drops, mapped

Each rung is engineered for between two and four explicit
dopamine drops. The drops are:

- **First-light** — the LED comes on. The cube renders. The
  exposure works. The print survives the wash. Always early in the
  rung.
- **The fix** — something doesn't work the way the reader expects;
  the rung explains why; the reader fixes it; relief.
- **The expansion** — the reader realises the thing they just
  built unlocks more than they thought. "Wait, I could also use
  this for X."
- **The signature** — the reader signs their work. End of the
  ladder.

Not every drop happens in every rung. The pacing matters: never
two drops in a row without a flat stretch between them.

## The studio's identity rule for courses

The reader, having finished a Holoflow ladder, should be able to
say one of the following with truth:

- "I am the kind of person who builds their own rigs."
- "I am the kind of person who signs my own prints."
- "I am the kind of person who runs my own AI locally."
- "I am the kind of person who edits my own splat captures."
- "I am the kind of person who calibrates my own colour."

The identity statement is the receipt. If the ladder doesn't
deliver it, the ladder isn't built right yet.

## Anti-patterns the studio specifically rejects

For clarity, in case the dark-pattern variants above were too
distributed:

- **Streak guilt.** No "you'll lose your streak" emails.
- **Notification fatigue.** No app-style pestering.
- **Fake urgency.** No "limited time only" countdowns.
- **Fake social proof.** No counterfeit reviews, no inflated
  enrolment numbers, no fake testimonials.
- **Bait paywalls.** Free content does not trail off into
  "subscribe to continue."
- **Comparison anxiety.** No leaderboards that shame slower
  learners.
- **Manipulative copy.** No "are you tired of being someone who…"
  sales pages.
- **Algorithmic upsell sequences.** Email drip campaigns with
  artificial intimacy are not used.
- **Cliffhangers that don't pay off.** Every named open thread
  is closed in the next rung.

If the studio breaks any of these, the principle is broken. The
reader notices. Trust never recovers.

## How to test whether a rung is doing it right

The checklist applied to every new rung before it ships:

- [ ] Reader has at least one explicit aha drop in the first
      third.
- [ ] At least five of the eighteen levers are engaged.
- [ ] Time-to-first-win is under thirty minutes.
- [ ] No dark-pattern technique appears anywhere.
- [ ] The closing cliffhanger is specific and is answered in the
      next rung.
- [ ] One implementation-intention prompt at the end.
- [ ] One reflection prompt requiring retrieval, not
      re-reading.
- [ ] The identity statement at end of the ladder is honestly
      earned by the work done across all rungs.
- [ ] Voice is held; no register slippage; no never-list words.
- [ ] Every claim anchored by a concrete number.

If any box is unticked, the rung is incomplete. Ship when all
ticked.

## The promise

The studio's contract with the reader, written here so it can be
held to:

> The Holoflow courses are designed to make you keep learning
> because the learning is genuinely working, not because the
> studio has engineered a habit-loop you can't escape. Every
> dopamine drop is paid for with a real skill acquired. Every
> open thread is closed. The streak you build is the rig in
> your kitchen, the prints on the wall, the scene running in
> your headset. There is no streak inside the studio's
> notification system, because the studio doesn't run one.

Apply this every rung. No exceptions.
