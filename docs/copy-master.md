## Statement

**Eyebrow:** `Statement`

**Headline:** `Gesture, captured. Gesture, cast.`

**Body:**

> Holo-Flow Studio began as twelve years of poi — the spun weight, the
> practised arc, fire held against the dark. The camera came later,
> to keep the gestures that would otherwise disappear at dawn. Then
> the gestures got programmed, precise, rendered in LED arrays built
> to draw whatever the hand could lift.
>
> The **photographs** are the record: single long exposures of image
> data written into physical space by moving light, captured whole.
> No compositing.
>
> The **objects** are what happens when those photographs go back
> into the physical. 3D-printed bodies whose geometry is derived from
> the captured trace, shot through with clear acrylic waveguides
> that carry internal light through the print. The gesture glows
> again from inside the body it drew.
>
> Each piece is editioned, signed, and ships with the field record —
> where the gesture was performed, what hour, what date.

## Pipeline

**Eyebrow:** `The pipeline`
**Headline:** `From a gesture in a field to an object on your shelf.`

Five stages:

1. **Perform** — A kata held in a place that matters. Fire poi, programmed LED arrays, drone-mounted lights. One exposure.
2. **Capture** — The photograph holds what the eye and the room saw. Sometimes it's the final object. Sometimes it's the seed.
3. **Translate** — The photograph becomes a mesh. Voxel tracing, extrusion, boolean passes. The gesture becomes geometry.
4. **Print** — SLA-printed in resin. Clear acrylic waveguides grown through the body. LEDs seated at the waveguide roots.
5. **Finish** — Sanded, tinted, polished. Seated on its base. Powered, addressed, numbered. Packed with its field record.

# /about

## Header

**Eyebrow:** `Document 000`
**Headline:** `The practice, the method, **the studio.**`

## I — The practice

**Headline:** `Twelve years of poi.`

> Poi is a movement discipline. Two weights on two cords, drawn
> through planes by the body. It takes years to make fluent — years
> before the weights stop ruling you and you start ruling them. I
> came to it for the dance, not the photograph.
>
> The first few thousand hours were spent on the geometry itself.
> Cross-follow, antispin, weaves, arm combinations. Fire poi at the
> end of the first year. After that, the body knew what a planar
> gesture was. It just couldn't *keep* one.
>
> Holo-Flow Studio is what happens when those trails are pulled out
> of the photograph and cast into something you can put on a shelf.

## II — The methods

**Headline:** `Three ways to paint light, one way to hold it.`

> The photographs come from three different light-painting practices,
> each contributing different qualities to the archive.
>
> **Traditional light painting.** Torches, LED wands, fire poi,
> handheld sticks. Organic, gestural, human-scale. Good for landscape
> pieces and for work where the body's presence in the gesture is
> part of the subject.
>
> **Persistence-of-vision LED arrays.** Addressable LEDs on a moving
> chassis — programmed frame by frame, synced against a rotation
> sensor, able to render arbitrary image data *as light, in physical
> space.* The rigs are built in-studio around a Teensy microcontroller
> and TLC5927 drivers; the frames are designed ahead in software;
> the body walks the spatial trajectory. What the camera captures is
> not the body — it is the image data in its briefly-real physical
> form.
>
> **Drone-mounted LED systems.** The studio's drone fleet (Mavic 2
> Pro, Neo, Neo 2, Avata 360) is in service for aerial photography
> today, flown FPV through DJI Goggles, an RC 2 controller, an InAir
> head-tracking pod, and Xreal One Pro AR glasses. The airframes
> have now been modified with programmable LED arrays for aerial
> light painting — rings at altitude, traces over landscape, arcs
> across architecture, geometries too large for any body. The
> platform is flying. First test captures recording this season.

Link: "The full history of how these stacked up →" → `/practice`

## III — The objects

**Headline:** `Photograph becomes geometry. Geometry becomes object.`

> The photographs are finished work in their own right, editioned and
> printed. But they are also source material. Each capture can be
> translated into 3D geometry — voxel-traced, extruded, refined as a
> mesh — and then SLA-printed in resin as an object.
>
> Embedded in the body of every object: clear acrylic **ambient-light
> waveguides**, grown along the trace lines of the original gesture.
> A small warm LED seated at the waveguide root carries light through
> the print, edge-lighting the geometry from inside. The gesture
> that was drawn in the dark glows again, from inside the body it
> drew.
>
> Three product lines: **desktop waveguides** (palm-scale, lit),
> **sculptures** (object-scale, lit or unlit), and **wall arrays**
> (configurable, commission).

## IV — The studio

**Headline:** `Manchester, UK.`

> Holo-Flow Studio is based in Manchester. Dimona Dougherty — founder,
> technologist, twelve years into poi — runs the practice. The studio
> is a small one: one pair of hands, one workshop, small editions.
>
> Everything is made to order, in runs. Nothing is stockpiled.
> Editions close on sell-through and never reopen. Each object
> carries its provenance: a card inside the shipping box naming the
> kata, the location, the hour, the date. If a track of music
> mattered to the gesture, it's named too.

# /practice

## Header

**Eyebrow:** `A history of the practice`
**Headline:** `How light learned to hold **a shape.**`

**Sub:**

> The tools changed. The question didn't. Each stage of the practice
> is a different answer to the same one: can a gesture last longer
> than the body that drew it?

## I — c. 2013 — Poi.

> Poi is a movement discipline: two weights on two cords, drawn
> through planes by the body. It takes a long time to make fluent —
> years before the weights stop ruling you and you start ruling
> them. I came to it for the dance, not the photograph.
>
> The first few thousand hours were spent on the geometry itself.
> Cross-follow, antispin, weaves, arm combinations. Fire poi at the
> end of the first year. After that, the body knew what a planar
> gesture was. It just couldn't *keep* one.

## II — c. 2015 — Long exposure.

> Someone pointed a camera. A fifteen-second exposure of a fire kata
> — one single still of a minute's worth of spinning — and the thing
> the body had known became visible to everyone else. That was the
> hinge. Dance left a trace; the trace was worth keeping.
>
> For the next few years, **traditional light painting** was the
> main tool: fire, LED wands, handheld torches, staff. Organic,
> gestural. A record of the body in motion. Still the practice most
> of the landscape photographs are made with.

## III — c. 2019 — Pixel poi.

> The commercial LED pois began arriving — pixel poi, addressable
> strips on a chassis that would read an image file and replay it,
> column by column, as the poi swung. Suddenly a kata could carry a
> *picture*. You could swing a parrot; you could swing a word. Most
> of the scene used them as a party trick. A few of us started
> treating them as instruments.
>
> What I wanted was to get the image quality up and the drift down.
> Commercial pois are designed for performance durability, not for
> photographic sharpness. So I started building.

## IV — c. 2021 — POV LED arrays.

> A **persistence-of-vision LED array** is a display that doesn't
> look like a display until it moves. A strip of addressable LEDs,
> a microcontroller, a rotation or translation mechanism, a
> synchronisation signal. When it moves, it draws — one vertical
> slice of image per angular sample, a thousand times a second. The
> eye integrates. The camera integrates too.
>
> My own rigs are built around a Teensy microcontroller, a bank of
> TLC5927 LED drivers, and Hall-effect sensors for rotational sync.
> One-hundred updates per revolution, at revolution rates the arm
> can sustain, means the image lands with photographic precision.
> I program the frames ahead of time; the body walks a trajectory;
> the long exposure captures the 3D volume the rig swept through.
>
> That's the Holo-Flow signature: a photograph that is not of
> anything that ever stood still.

## V — 2025 → — Drones, modified.

> The next move is up. The drone fleet is already in service for
> aerial photography — a Mavic 2 Pro for the photography-grade
> glass, a pair of Neos for follow-cam, an Avata 360 for FPV
> cinewhoop work, all flown through goggles and an RC 2 controller,
> head-tracked through an InAir pod with the live feed mirrored to
> Xreal One Pro AR glasses. Real flights, not hover-and-shoot.
>
> The current build mounts **programmable LED arrays onto the
> airframes**: the rig freed from the body, able to draw at
> altitude, across landscape, around architecture. Think a ring of
> light held thirty metres above a moor at 04:30, tracing a kata
> too large for any performer.
>
> The platform is operational. The LED-modified airframes are
> flying. *First test captures this evening.*

## VI — 2025 → — The objects.

> The photographs became the source for a new line: 3D-printed
> objects whose geometry is *derived* from the captured light. A
> gesture that was drawn in air becomes a sculpture cast in resin.
>
> Embedded in the body of every object: clear acrylic **ambient-light
> waveguides**, grown along the trace lines of the original gesture.
> A small LED seated at the waveguide root carries warm light
> through the print, edge-lighting the geometry from inside. The
> gesture that the body drew in the dark glows again from inside
> the object it made.
>
> Desktop pieces, wall arrays, and one-off sculptures — every one
> an object of the photograph it was grown from.

## Summary

> Holo-Flow Studio is the apparatus for holding gestures. Poi trained
> the body. Long exposure trained the camera. Pixel-level programming
> trained the eye to what light can carry. The objects are what
> those trainings make possible together: a sculpture, in a room, lit
> from inside by the residue of a gesture performed on a specific
> date, in a specific place, at a specific hour, held long enough to
> leave a mark.

## The fleet

**Headline:** `Four airframes, one pipeline.`

- **DJI Mavic 2 Pro** — Hasselblad L1D-20c, 1" sensor, adjustable aperture. The photography platform: stills and motion at editorial quality.
- **DJI Neo & Neo 2** — lightweight follow-cam and proximity work; useful indoors and around people.
- **DJI Avata 360** — FPV cinewhoop with an integrated 8K 360° camera (twin 64MP 1/1.1" sensors, 200° lenses). Single-lens mode for traditional FPV at 4K, dual-lens for full omnidirectional capture.
- **LED-modified airframes** — programmable persistence-of-vision LED arrays mounted to drone bodies, for aerial light painting kata. Currently in first-flight testing.

## Flown FPV, properly

**Headline:** `The control pipeline.`

> DJI Goggles + RC 2 controller, head-tracked through an InAir pod,
> with the live feed mirrored to Xreal One Pro AR glasses. The
> result: simultaneous gimbal control, AR-overlaid telemetry, and
> external situational awareness without losing line of sight to
> the airframe. Real flights, not hover-and-shoot.

## What it's good for

**Headline:** `What the studio takes on.`

- **Editorial / portraiture aerial.** Mavic 2 Pro, single-shot or short series, framed and graded for print.
- **FPV cinewhoop fly-throughs.** Avata 360 in 360 mode for venue interiors, processions, gigs — shot once, reframed in post.
- **Aerial light painting commissions.** LED-modified airframes performing programmed light kata over your site at night. Output is a print or an editioned photograph.
- **360° immersive capture.** Avata 360 for VR / virtual-tour applications.

## How it works

**Headline:** `Brief, fly, deliver.`

1. **Brief via the contact form** — location, intent, deliverables, dates. Pick "Aerial / drone work" from the form's intent dropdown.
2. **Quote and recce.** The studio replies with a quote, a recommended airframe for the job, and a proposed shoot window. Sometimes a recce visit; sometimes just a satellite read.
3. **Fly.** Manchester base, willing to travel. All flights operated to UK CAA rules and local site conditions. Insurance and op-id available on request.
4. **Deliver.** Graded stills as TIFF/JPEG, edited motion as ProRes/H.265, 360° clips as equirectangular. Or, for light-painting commissions, a signed editioned print.

# /shop/certificate

**Eyebrow:** `Policy`
**Headline:** `Certificate of authenticity`

> Every print released ships with a single certificate on 300 gsm
> cotton rag, embossed with the studio seal and hand-signed.
>
> The certificate records six facts: the work code, the edition
> reference, the edition number, the coordinates of the
> performance, the hour and date of the kata, and the studio's
> commitment that no further impressions of this edition will ever
> be made.
>
> The certificate is the authoritative record. Works without their
> certificate are outside the archive. The archive maintains a
> private ledger of certificate numbers against owners, held only
> for the purpose of confirming provenance on resale.

## On reprints

> No edition is re-opened. No work is re-issued in a different
> size, on a different paper, or under a different code. Two
> artist proofs are retained per edition and are not for sale.

> Works ship within 3–5 working days of order, rolled in an
> acid-free archival tube or, for objects, crated to size. All
