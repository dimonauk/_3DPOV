At 04:47 on the longest morning of the year, the Thames is not a river so
much as a corridor of cooling air running between two rows of lamps. The
kata called **Dawn Turn** takes forty-nine revolutions &mdash; seven
sevens &mdash; and ends the instant the first sun crosses the iron
railing at the eastern end of the walk.

The choreography is plain: a single rotation held at walking pace, the
arms carrying the lamp-rig at shoulder height, the body stepping one
river-length for each turn. The lamps record what the body draws. The
plate records what the lamps recorded.

The point of the Protocol is not the gesture. The point is what the
Embankment remembers of the gesture after the body has gone. On this
morning, the river answered by holding the trace for the length of one
held breath. The plate is that breath.
