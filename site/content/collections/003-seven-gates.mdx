The medieval wall of London had seven gates. Most of them are absent
now: a street name, a kerb stone, a doorway into a glass building whose
tenants do not know what they replaced. The kata for this collection is
**Walking Circle**, performed once at each gate, once per dusk, for
seven consecutive evenings in the week of the autumn equinox.

Each evening's trace is a single ring of light. The plate is the seven
rings in registration &mdash; the city's old boundary re-drawn by a body
that walked it slow enough for the lamps to find the shape. Where the
rings overlap, the plate darkens. Where a gate is gone, the ring is
broken. The absences are the point.

This is the largest edition in the Protocol at the time of writing, and
the one that asks the most of the room it hangs in.
